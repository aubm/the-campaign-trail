The Forgotten City

Morning · on the heights above Omu

The jungle stretched as far as the eye could see, shrouded in the early morning mist. Beneath the canopy, the last nocturnal insects were giving way to birdsong. The air was already warm, heavy with moisture and the scent of vegetation.

A half-elf with long black hair tied back walked at the head of the group, a few paces behind their guide. He pushed a branch aside with his forearm and stopped abruptly.

“You should come and see this.”

Salida turned. A faint smile crossed her face.

“We’re here.”

Before them, the forest ended at the edge of a cliff.

An entire city lay below.

Hundreds of limestone buildings rose out of the vegetation. Avenues vanished beneath tree roots, broken columns stood above the foliage, and farther off, the remains of an immense circular palace towered over the ruins. A thin mist still drifted between the buildings, pierced by the first rays of sunlight.

The jungle had swallowed Omu, but it had not erased it.

For several seconds, no one spoke.

“By Deneir…” Talia breathed.

The cartographer approached the edge cautiously and took out her instruments.

“Erdan, this is impossible. The maps we found didn’t do it justice.”

“That’s because none of the people who drew them had ever set foot here,” Erdan replied.

Talia found his hand and gave it a brief squeeze. They exchanged a glance, then looked back at the city.

His gaze travelled over the streets, the buildings, the temples rising above the trees.

He had devoted years to this search. Incomplete manuscripts, contradictory maps, entire nights spent comparing inscriptions whose meaning had long been lost. More than once, he had thought he was close. More than once, he had had to start again.

And now the city was there.

Darek, the guard hired to protect them, set his heavy pack on the ground.

“All that for a few piles of stones?”

“Piles of stones that are centuries old,” Talia corrected him.

“Ah. In that case. All that for a few centuries-old piles of stones?”

Ghaar, the youngest member of the expedition, burst out laughing.

Erdan had already stopped listening. He had crouched down with his field notebook on his knees and was trying to identify the main landmarks.

“The main avenue… The palace… And there, look at that building to the east.”

Maeren came to stand beside him.

“Do you think that’s the sanctuary?”

“I don’t know yet.”

Erdan traced the course of a street half hidden by vegetation with his finger.

“But at last we have somewhere to look.”

Maeren laid a hand on his shoulder.

“You were right.”

Erdan looked up.

“About what?”

“All of this. Even when no one else believed this city was still here.”

Erdan smiled without answering.

A little way behind them, Salida watched. Her gaze moved from the explorers to the city, then lingered on Erdan.

“We should head down,” she said. “The heat will be much harder to bear once the sun is high.”

“And the dangers?” Darek asked.

“The same as anywhere else in Chult.”

She paused briefly.

“With a few extra surprises.”

Darek sighed and hoisted his pack onto his shoulders.

Erdan stood to take one last look at the city.

He felt the exhilaration of a great discovery, but also a strange unease. Something about the silent vastness of the ruins made him feel as though he were being watched.

He pushed the thought aside.

“Let’s go.”

And the six travellers began their descent towards Omu.

Beneath the Stones

Afternoon · among the ruins

The sun had reached its zenith by the time the expedition ventured into the central districts.

The heat seemed trapped between the walls. Vines hung from ancient façades, moss covered the uneven paving stones, and banyan roots tore through the buildings.

Erdan moved slowly, stopping often to examine an architectural detail or copy an inscription.

“We’ve passed three shrines since this morning,” Talia said. “Would you like us to study all of them?”

“Ideally.”

“We don’t have three years.”

“Two would probably be enough.”

She gave him an exasperated look. Erdan smiled and kept walking.

Near a small square, Ghaar stopped in front of a partially collapsed building.

“Erdan? You need to look at this.”

A carved frieze ran along the façade. Vegetation covered much of it, but some of the motifs were still visible: kneeling human figures, animals, and a series of signs shaped like mazes.

Erdan ran his fingers gently over the stone.

“It’s the same arrangement of symbols as on the fragments in the library at Port Nyanzaru.”

“Are you sure?” Maeren asked.

“Not yet. Ghaar, bring me my notebook.”

The young man rummaged in Erdan’s satchel and handed him the book.

For several minutes, they compared the drawings.

“Look,” Erdan said, pointing to two motifs. “The same lines. The same arrangement.”

He turned the notebook towards Ghaar.

“So, what can we conclude?”

The young man studied the two drawings, as he had been doing for months under Erdan’s guidance.

“That we’re probably not looking in the wrong place?”

“Exactly. A lead, not proof. Not yet.”

Ghaar smiled. For once, he had anticipated the lesson.

They entered the building.

Inside, light barely filtered through the gaps between the ceiling stones. An enormous tree grew in an inner courtyard, its roots lifting sections of the paving.

Darek stayed by the entrance while Talia and Ghaar began clearing away the rubble.

Erdan noticed a particularly large slab, almost entirely buried in earth.

“Wait. This one doesn’t belong here.”

“What makes you say that?” Talia asked.

“The way it’s aligned. And the tool marks along the edges. It wasn’t part of the original floor.”

He knelt and began clearing the earth from around it.

“Darek, can you give us a hand?”

“I was beginning to wonder why you were paying me.”

After several attempts, they tipped the slab aside.

A breath of cold air rose from the opening.

Beneath their feet, a stone staircase descended into darkness.

The group fell silent.

Maeren approached slowly, one hand held out towards the passage. A faint glow formed between his fingers.

His expression changed at once.

“Erdan.”

“What?”

“There’s something down there.”

“We can see that.”

“No. I mean magic.”

The mage took a step back.

“A tremendous concentration of it. I’ve never felt anything like it in ruins this old.”

Erdan moved closer to the opening.

Fine patterns were carved into the first few steps. They resembled the symbols in the texts that had led them to Omu.

He took out his notebook and began to sketch.

“We’ve been searching for this place for years…” he murmured.

Talia laid a hand on his arm. Erdan covered her fingers with his own for a moment.

“Erdan, we don’t know what’s down there.”

“I know.”

“And Maeren looks worried.”

The mage was still watching the opening.

“This place should have been dead long ago. I don’t understand how such powerful magic can still linger here.”

Erdan hesitated.

Everything in him urged him to go down. He was so close to finally understanding why so many ancient texts spoke of this place with an almost religious dread.

But Talia was right.

“We’ll carry out a full survey tomorrow morning,” he decided. “We’ll go down once we’ve prepared the equipment we need.”

Ghaar protested.

“After all these years, are we really going to wait until tomorrow?”

“After all these years,” Erdan replied, “one more night shouldn’t kill us.”

Darek gave a quiet laugh.

“At last, something sensible.”

In the shadows by the entrance, Salida had watched the entire discovery without a word.

“I know a sheltered courtyard a little farther on,” she offered. “You can make camp there.”

Erdan nodded.

“All right. We’ll come back first thing.”

As the others gathered their tools, the guide stepped away.

Hidden behind a stretch of wall, she discreetly opened her pouch and took out a small polished stone.

She raised it to her lips.

“They’ve found it.”

The stone glimmered faintly in her hand.

Then the light went out.

The Last Fire

Evening · a ruined courtyard in the heart of Omu

They had made camp in the courtyard of an old house, enclosed on three sides by limestone walls that still stood.

A fire burned in the centre. Above it, a small pot was struggling to warm the last meal of the day.

The explorers were tired and covered in dust, but in excellent spirits.

Darek stirred the contents of the pot with a suspicious look.

“We’ve crossed half of Chult, escaped shivering sickness, mad monkey fever and dinosaurs… only to end up poisoned by these blasted rations.”

“They’re the same rations we had last week,” Ghaar pointed out.

“Exactly.”

Talia sat beside Erdan, finishing a map of the ruins on a large sheet of paper.

“If that passage really leads to what we’re looking for, we’ll have enough to keep generations of archaeologists busy.”

“Let’s not sell the dinosaur’s hide before we’ve killed it,” Erdan replied.

“I thought you didn’t want to kill dinosaurs.”

“I prefer to wait until they die of old age, Talia. They bite a lot less afterwards.”

She smiled, gave him a quick kiss, then went back to her drawing as though nothing had happened. Erdan slipped an arm around her waist, still studying the map.

A little farther away, Maeren gazed into the flames.

“There’s something I don’t like about that sanctuary.”

Darek rolled his eyes.

“Here we go.”

“I’m serious.”

Erdan turned towards the mage.

“Did you sense something dangerous?”

“I don’t know. That’s what worries me.”

He rubbed his hands together for warmth, despite the lingering heat.

“It’s not just the amount of magic. It’s as though… something is holding it in place. As though that sanctuary never stopped functioning.”

“Then we’ll be careful,” Erdan replied. “And we won’t touch anything until we know what we’re dealing with.”

“Promise?”

Erdan smiled.

“I can be very sensible.”

Talia burst out laughing.

“That’s the best promise we’ll get out of him.”

Their laughter mingled with the crackle of the fire.

For a moment, Erdan watched his companions.

He knew their faults, their habits, their petty arguments. He had shared months of travel with them, fruitless searches and small discoveries that, taken together, had finally brought them here.

Tomorrow, perhaps it would all finally make sense.

Then he noticed Salida standing apart, near the entrance to the courtyard.

“Aren’t you eating with us?”

The guide turned her head.

“Later. I’m checking the surroundings.”

“Is something wrong?”

“Why do you ask?”

Erdan hesitated.

“You’ve been very quiet since we found the passage.”

She looked at him for a few seconds.

“Some discoveries deserve silence, Erdan.”

Then she disappeared behind the wall.

He stood puzzled for a moment before turning back to his companions.

That was when he noticed something.

The insects had stopped singing.

He looked up.

“Darek?”

The guard turned.

“What?”

A projectile cut through the darkness.

Darek clutched his neck, staggered, then collapsed beside the fire.

“Darek!”

Erdan rushed to him.

A second dart struck the ground.

Then a third.

“TAKE COVER!” Maeren shouted.

Figures emerged from the ruins. Humanoids with reptilian features, armed with curved blades and blowguns. Others moved between the walls with an almost animal suppleness.

Maeren raised his hands.

A fierce light erupted before him, driving two attackers back. One was hurled against a column.

“Run!” the mage screamed.

Ghaar pulled Talia towards a collapsed archway.

Erdan tried to lift Darek, but the guard no longer responded. Blood ran between his fingers.

“Darek! Look at me!”

A hiss sounded behind him.

Erdan turned just in time to see an enormous snake coming down a wall.

He let go of Darek and scrambled back.

Maeren intervened.

Another burst of light struck the creature, but an attacker had already appeared behind the mage.

The blade pierced his chest.

Maeren went still.

His hands slowly dropped.

Then he crumpled.

“NO!”

Erdan got to his feet, snatched the small dagger from his belt and rushed towards him.

Talia caught his arm.

“Erdan! We have to go!”

“We have to help them!”

“They’re dead!”

He tore himself free.

“DON’T SAY THAT!”

A cry rang out behind them.

Ghaar had fallen, struck as he tried to climb a low wall. Two creatures leapt upon him.

Talia pulled Erdan back.

“Come on!”

They ran towards a narrow exit, but another figure blocked their path.

The man before them had a humanoid torso and held a long sword. Below the waist, his body was that of an immense serpent, covered in dark red scales.

The other attackers moved aside to let him pass.

Talia stopped short.

The being regarded them with terrifying composure.

“So. Our visitors.”

Erdan backed away, his fingers tight around his dagger.

“Who are you?”

The serpent-man did not answer at once.

His gaze settled on Erdan, then dropped to the field notebook hanging from his belt.

“Is this the archaeologist?”

A familiar voice came from the darkness.

“Yes, my lord.”

Salida entered the courtyard.

Erdan stared at her in disbelief.

“Salida?”

The guide approached the serpent-man and inclined her head.

“Erdan Naïlo. He’s the one who found the passage.”

“Excellent.”

Erdan stared into her face.

“You knew what was waiting for us?”

“I brought you where you needed to go,” she replied.

He took a step towards her, but Talia caught his hand.

“Erdan, no.”

The serpent-bodied warrior’s gaze followed the gesture.

“I am Ras Nsi,” he said at last. “And what you have discovered does not belong to you.”

He turned to his men.

“The others are of no use to us.”

Two yuan-ti seized Erdan by the arms. His dagger fell onto the paving stones.

Talia rushed towards him, but a third warrior stopped her and forced her to her knees. The tip of a blade came to rest against her throat.

“Let her go!” Erdan shouted.

He struggled so violently that it took three soldiers to hold him.

“I’m the one you want! She hasn’t done anything!”

Talia sought his gaze despite the blade at her neck.

“Erdan… Don’t do this.”

“Don’t touch her!” he repeated. “Please.”

Ras Nsi approached him. With one finger, he slowly traced the curve of his cheek, then brushed aside a strand of his hair as though soothing a frightened animal. Erdan turned his head away in disgust.

But Ras Nsi was no longer looking at his face.

He was watching the way Erdan’s eyes kept seeking Talia, the way he tensed at every movement of the blade against her skin.

The yuan-ti raised his sword to strike her down. Erdan cried out and tried desperately to throw himself between them.

Then Ras Nsi raised his hand.

“Wait… She may yet prove useful.”

End of the prologue.
