## The Last Days at Sea

*End of the voyage · approaching Port Nyanzaru*

Jana had stopped counting the days when they had all begun to look the same.

In the morning, the deck was damp. At noon, the sun made the ropes too hot to touch. In the evening, the sailors spoke of a wind that might change overnight, only to wake and announce that it had not.

The Wisdom of the Seas sailed on. After weeks at sea, Port Nyanzaru was now close.

The wind brushed her face, carrying the salt smell of the spray. Beneath her feet, the steady slap of waves against the hull mingled with the creak of the rigging.

Since leaving Baldur's Gate, Jana had grown used to the roll of the ship. Not to the time slipping away. Every day that brought her nearer to Chult was another day Celeborn spent waiting, far behind her.

Near the rail, Adwayria was watching the water.

“Do you think they've been following us for long?”

Jana looked towards the surface.

“Who?”

“The fish.”

A pale shape had just vanished beneath the hull. Jana had seen nothing but a ripple.

“I don't know.”

“They might have something to tell us.”

Jana thought of the rat among the warehouses, the blow Doran said she had taken on her fingers, and the argument that had followed on the quay.

“Ask them when they come back.”

Adwayria leaned over again, perfectly serious.

A little farther away, Zarraï was reading through a notebook. He had found a spot out of the crew's way, though not quite apart from the bustle of the deck. Whenever someone shouted an order, he paused, waited for the passage to clear, then returned to his reading.

Jana knew him well enough to have learnt that he spoke little, but not well enough to know what he was writing.

Doran, Bran's first mate, strode across the deck.

“The captain expects all three of you this evening. In his cabin. Don't be late.”

“Why?” Adwayria asked.

“Because he asked you to be on time,” Doran replied curtly.

She carried on before Adwayria could think of another question.

Zarraï closed his notebook.

“What a warm invitation.”

“Do you think she's still angry with me?” Adwayria asked.

“Oh, not at all, my dear,” Zarraï replied with the utmost seriousness. “I wouldn't worry. She's positively overflowing with affection for you.”

Adwayria was reassured. Jana smiled.

She wondered whom else Bran had invited. The dwarf had spoken to her about a friend in Chult, contacts at the port, and a lead on finding Artus Cimber. He had never explained why so many travellers on the same ship seemed to need to reach the same place.

## The Captain's Table

*Evening · Bran's cabin*

Bran greeted them with the door open and his sleeves rolled up.

A table filled almost the entire cabin. Maps were piled on a shelf, held in place by a brass instrument. Between two chests, a white wolf lay on the floor, her head resting on her paws.

Adwayria stopped on the threshold.

“Oh.”

Bran followed her gaze.

“Meet Sagesse.”

“She's beautiful,” Adwayria whispered.

“She knows it. She's already cost me several mouthfuls of my dinner.”

The wolf raised one ear without moving.

“Sagesse, these are the people who are going to try to let me eat in peace.”

Adwayria crouched at once. She held out a hand and waited. The wolf sniffed it, then brought her head closer.

Bran pulled out a chair.

“I can see you're getting along already. Try not to fight over my cabin.”

Jana sat down near the door. Zarraï chose a seat from which he could see both Bran and the other guests.

One chair remained empty.

A wet chewing sound came from somewhere above the bread basket.

Jana looked first at the wolf. Sagesse had raised her head and was staring at something behind Bran's arm.

A small creature clung to the shelf. Membranous wings were folded against its back; its fingers were too long, and its wrinkled face surrounded a mouth full of sharp teeth. A crust of bread protruded from its jaws. It chewed diligently, apparently oblivious to the guests.

Adwayria's eyes widened.

“What is that?”

The creature looked at her, then fixed Jana with a moist-eyed stare.

“Maaaaster… There are ladies at the captain's table.”

Its rasping voice sounded like a gurgle forced into the shape of words.

“And guests who were planning to eat that bread before you,” Bran pointed out.

A voice came from the back of the cabin.

“Ghaar. Put that down. Come here.”

A man stood before the maps. Jana had not noticed him when she came in. His black hair was tied back, with a few strands falling around a dark-bearded face. A small silver key hung around his neck.

Ghaar tightened his grip on the crust.

“But Maaaaster, nobody wanted it…”

“I didn't ask for an explanation. What do you say?”

The creature slowly folded its wings.

“Sooorry, Maaaaster.”

“And now?”

Ghaar deposited what remained of the crust in the outstretched hand. The bread bore the impressions of his teeth and dripped with thick saliva.

The stranger regarded it with satisfaction, then carefully returned it to the basket among the untouched pieces.

“Please excuse his bad manners. He's still learning how to behave.”

Bran froze with his hand above the basket. Jana exchanged a glance with Zarraï, who quietly moved his plate away.

Ghaar climbed onto his master's shoulder, head bowed. The man stroked the top of his skull fondly.

“There. You can be very well behaved when you want to. Isn't he adorable?”

A thread of saliva ran onto the man's collar. He seemed not to notice.

“Er… yes… He's certainly… one of a kind,” Adwayria replied, plainly searching for words.

The man looked at Ghaar with a disconcerting mixture of almost proud tenderness and quiet contempt.

“A marvel,” he declared, evidently convinced.

He turned towards the other guests.

“You must be the others.”

No one answered at once.

He smiled, more broadly than the occasion seemed to warrant.

“How curious. We've been travelling on the same ship, and it's taken until tonight for us to be introduced.”

“Which is precisely what I intend to put right. Erdan, meet Jana, Adwayria and Zarraï,” Bran interjected.

Erdan bowed without taking his eyes off the three travellers.

“Delighted.”

“As are we,” Jana replied.

“Did you board at Baldur's Gate with us?” Adwayria asked.

“Yes.”

“I didn't see you.”

“I heard you, though,” Erdan retorted.

He glanced at the staff she had propped against her chair.

Adwayria lifted her chin.

“That rat wasn't hurting anyone.”

“I don't doubt it for a second,” Erdan assured her.

The seriousness of his reply disarmed her. Then he gave a short laugh, as though he had only just pictured the scene.

Adwayria could not decide whether to be offended or curious.

“Do sit down, Erdan. You can discuss matters with the rats at our next port of call. But do try to keep your companion away from my plates,” Bran concluded.

“Ghaar, you heard the captain. Not another finger in the food.”

“Yeees, Maaaaster.”

Bran pushed the bread basket beyond his reach.

Dinner arrived. Bran served his guests without ceremony, handed out bread, called for another jug, then waited until everyone had begun eating.

“You're probably wondering why I've brought you together.”

“I imagine it isn't solely for the cooking,” Zarraï remarked.

“My cooking is excellent. But no,” Bran replied.

He set down his glass.

“In Port Nyanzaru, I intend to introduce you to Syndra Silvaine. She asked some of you to come; others are travelling for their own reasons. I've known her a long time, and I think you'll all benefit from working together.”

Jana looked around the table.

“I don't know this Syndra. Do you think she'll be able to help us?”

“Syndra knows my family. She wrote to me. She said it was important,” Adwayria explained.

“She never writes without a reason,” Erdan said.

His voice had changed. The smile was gone.

“Do you know her well?” Jana asked.

“Well enough to owe her a great deal.”

He spoke with such theatrical solemnity that Jana wondered for a moment whether he was joking. Yet he gripped his glass with absolute seriousness.

“She saved my life. I don't intend to forget it.”

A silence followed. Bran put a plate beside him.

“Eat, Erdan. You can express your gratitude when we're ashore.”

Erdan looked at the plate, then at Bran. He nodded, as though he needed a moment to find his place in the evening again.

“Yes. Of course.”

Adwayria was still stroking the wolf with her fingertips.

“What about you, Jana? Why are you going?”

Jana lowered her spoon.

“I'm looking for Artus Cimber. Bran told me he'd met him there.”

“I also told her that meeting wasn't exactly yesterday,” Bran reminded them.

“I know,” Jana replied.

She hesitated. Telling the dwarf about Celeborn one night at the Elfsong had been hard enough. Telling three strangers was another matter.

“Someone dear to me is ill. I have to find a way to help him.”

For a moment, Zarraï's fingers tightened around his glass. He relaxed them immediately without looking up.

Adwayria gently withdrew her hand from the white fur.

“Can he be healed?”

“Not by anything I know,” Jana answered.

“Then I hope we'll find something,” Adwayria said.

Jana looked at her. She had neither asked for details nor promised the impossible.

“Thank you.”

Bran turned to Zarraï.

“As for our friend, I've dealt with his family before.”

“And I'd like it to remain a good business relationship,” Zarraï replied.

“You see? Always thinking of sparing my feelings,” Bran said.

“That still doesn't tell us what you're looking for in Chult,” Erdan observed.

Zarraï set down his glass.

“Something I can't find in Baldur's Gate.”

“And you think you'll find it there?” Erdan asked.

“I intend to try,” Zarraï replied.

Erdan held his gaze a second too long. Then he leaned back and smiled again.

“A cautious answer. Perhaps I should have started with one myself.”

Bran gave them time to resume their meal.

“I don't know what Syndra will be able to tell you. And I won't pry your secrets out of you at my table. But if you're going to cross Chult together, I'd rather you exchanged more than your names before setting foot ashore.”

“You think we'll have to leave Port Nyanzaru?” Jana asked.

“I think that once you're there, you'll better understand what Syndra can offer. As for the rest, she'll tell you herself,” Bran said.

He indicated the maps behind Erdan.

“That country deserves to be taken seriously. The people who claim to know it after a single stop are usually the ones who've never ventured beyond the taverns at the port.”

“And have you been beyond the taverns?” Adwayria asked.

“Oh, certainly. I've even made it as far as the customs offices,” Bran replied.

Adwayria frowned. Jana suppressed a laugh.

Erdan, meanwhile, had turned towards the maps. Chult's coasts were drawn in painstaking detail, but inland the names and lines grew sparse. Vast stretches were almost blank. He traced a road that ended well before the heart of the island.

“Ruins, vanished cities, things forgotten for centuries…” Erdan murmured.

“There must be so much left to discover.”

“Are you an explorer?” Jana asked.

“An archaeologist,” Erdan replied without hesitation.

The silver key slipped out of his collar as he sat down again. He tucked it back beneath his shirt.

Jana was about to ask which ruins he had already explored when hurried footsteps sounded above their heads.

Bran looked up.

Another sound followed, then a voice none of them could make out.

The ship's bell began to ring.

## A Sail on the Horizon

*Nightfall · off Port Nyanzaru*

Doran opened the door without knocking.

“Bran. A ship's coming towards us.”

The captain was already on his feet.

“Her flag?” Bran asked.

“None I recognise,” Doran replied.

Bran took his spyglass from a shelf and headed for the deck. His guests followed. Sagesse slipped between their legs, then climbed up behind her master.

Outside, the western sky was still light. A dark silhouette was gaining on them rapidly across the water.

The sailors had abandoned their ordinary conversations. Orders travelled from one end of the deck to the other; a rope was retied, a crate moved, a better place sought from which to watch the approaching ship.

Bran raised the spyglass to his eye.

He stood motionless.

“Well?” Doran pressed.

“Zaroum,” Bran said.

Doran turned sharply towards him.

“Who's that?” Jana asked.

Bran lowered the spyglass.

“A pirate. And certainly not someone I was hoping to invite to dinner.”

“You know him?” Zarraï asked.

“Well enough to know he isn't here to ask for directions,” Bran replied.

He pointed to the figure standing on the other deck, some distance from the men at work on the rigging.

“There he is. But it's his men who'll be coming over to us.”

As the two ships drew closer, Jana began to make out armed figures, then blades catching the last rays of daylight.

Adwayria could not take her eyes off the other ship.

“Are they going to come aboard?”

“If they can,” Doran retorted.

The first mate called an order to her sailors.

Bran turned to his passengers.

“You didn't sign on to fight my enemies. If you can stay under cover, do.”

A blow rang against the hull, followed by a shout.

The spyglass disappeared into the captain's pocket.

“Everyone to your posts!” Bran ordered.

## The First Assault

*Moments later · between the two ships*

The first grappling hook struck the deck with a clang of iron.

A sailor rushed to free the rope. Another hook caught the wood farther away, and the enemy vessel gave theirs a sudden pull.

Jana caught herself against the rail.

Figures moved on the other deck. One of the pirates leapt across, landed on one knee aboard the Wisdom of the Seas and immediately raised his weapon. Behind him, others were already looking for a way over.

“Drive them back!” Doran shouted.

The din almost drowned out her order.

A sailor fell beneath a blow. Jana ran towards him, but another pirate blocked her path. She raised her hand.

“Lathander, protect them!”

A flash of light struck the pirate. He stumbled back, one arm shielding his face. Jana passed him, knelt beside the sailor and saw blood soaking through his sleeve.

“Leave me. They're coming back,” the sailor gasped.

“Not a chance,” Jana said.

She placed her palm against the wound. A golden warmth passed between her fingers; the bleeding slowed. When the sailor managed to sit up, she took his arm and led him back behind the other defenders.

Farther away, two men from the enemy ship were pulling on a rope attached to a grappling hook. A third was preparing a plank to span the gap between the hulls. From the other deck, a pirate directed the operation, signalling when they should pull the rope taut.

Zarraï had backed towards a wooden post. He watched the men's movements, then the rope tightening with every roll of the ships. Doran shouted that they had to hold the stern. As long as the hook remained in place, the pirates could draw the two vessels together.

An armed man was guarding the end of the rope a few paces from Zarraï. He could not reach it without being noticed.

Zarraï looked up at the pirate directing the manoeuvre on the other ship.

His lips barely moved.

Amid the clash of weapons, nobody seemed to hear the few discordant notes he murmured. Nobody except his target.

The pirate clapped his hands to his head. He staggered as though something unbearable were whispering in his ear. Then he turned and fled towards the stern of his own ship, as far from Zarraï as the deck would allow.

The two men holding the rope looked for their leader. They kept pulling all the same. The armed pirate watching Zarraï turned away as well, startled by the inexplicable flight.

Zarraï seized his chance. He drew his rapier and slipped up to the still-taut rope. He slid the slender blade beneath several strands and pulled sharply, just as the hulls drifted apart.

The rope snapped.

The plank being pushed between the ships tipped before it could be secured. The pirates had to jump back to avoid following it into the water. A gap opened in the boarding assault, long enough for Doran to bring several sailors into position.

Zarraï let the cut end of the rope fall. His sleeve had slipped; he immediately pulled it back down to his wrist.

“Well done!” Doran shouted.

He answered with a small nod.

Near the mainmast, Ghaar spotted two pirates who had managed to climb aboard from the opposite side.

“Maaaaster! Behind! Behind them!”

Erdan, who had remained near the stairs, spun round at once.

“I see them, Ghaar. Keep that one busy.”

The familiar gave a rough cackle and swooped down upon the nearer attacker. He clung to the man's hair, spread his wings over his eyes and, to block his sight completely, planted his rear end against the pirate's nose.

The man screamed, dropped his weapon and groped to tear the creature from his face. Even Jana, several paces away, caught a smell rather less pleasant than the sea air.

Erdan took advantage of the distraction. He raised his hand.

A crackling bolt of energy zigzagged through the air and struck the pirate squarely in the chest. The man arched under the impact; flinging out his arms to recover his balance, he sent Ghaar flying across the deck. The small winged body finished its journey against a crate with a dull thud.

“Very nicely done, Maaaaster…”

Ghaar tried to get up, his snout crooked and his wings crumpled.

Erdan gave a sudden laugh, almost delighted.

“Ha! You've chosen the wrong ship!”

He had already turned his attention to the second pirate, who was retreating behind a crate, without looking to see where his familiar had landed.

“Maaaaster… my wings…”

“You're very brave, my little Ghaar. Truly remarkable,” Erdan declared.

He spoke with affectionate pride, yet did not spare him a glance. Still unsteady, Ghaar made his painful way back to his shoulder.

The first pirates were falling back. But several had regrouped near the bow, where the rail left little room to fight. One knocked a sailor to the deck. Two more advanced behind him.

Doran stepped between them and the defenders, her weapon raised. The first pirate feigned retreat before striking her beneath the arm. The half-orc faltered. She turned towards Adwayria, seemingly surprised to find her there, then fell to her knees in front of her.

Adwayria tried to support her. Doran's legs gave way; she collapsed against the druid. Blood ran between Adwayria's fingers as she tried to lift the first mate's head.

“Doran?”

The first mate did not answer. Her gaze was fixed. Adwayria called her name again, louder this time.

Ahead of them, the pirates were still advancing.

Adwayria sprang to her feet, leaving Doran stretched out on the deck.

“GET BACK!”

She planted her feet on the planks and held out both hands.

The air seemed to draw tight before her.

A thunderclap erupted, so violent that Jana felt its force in her chest. Ropes whipped across the deck; an abandoned bucket went rolling. The pirates were hurled backwards. Three of them went over the rail and vanished into the water.

The silence that followed lasted barely a second.

“By the gods!” a sailor exclaimed.

Adwayria stared at the ship's edge, her hands still outstretched. Then her eyes returned to Doran, still lying at her feet.

She knelt without a word. Jana joined her, pressed two fingers to the half-orc's neck and raised her eyes to Adwayria. The look was answer enough.

Another attacker appeared. Jana took Adwayria by the shoulder and pulled her towards the middle of the deck.

“Adwayria. Don't stay there!”

Bran's sailors regained some of the ground they had lost. On the enemy vessel, Zaroum had not moved from his post. He was still watching, beyond their reach, while his men tried to regroup.

“Don't pursue them! Hold the deck!” Bran ordered.

Jana returned to the others. She counted them almost without meaning to: Adwayria, Zarraï, Erdan. Ghaar was still clinging to Erdan's shoulder.

“Have we won?” Adwayria asked.

The answer came from another direction.

A rumble was rising from beneath the sea.

At first Jana thought it was the sound of the two hulls scraping together. But it went on after the ships had stopped moving against one another.

The deck trembled beneath her feet.

Then the shouting ceased, one cry at a time.

## What Rises from Below

*Evening · the sea between the ships*

In the sudden silence, the wind went on filling the sails.

Jana heard water slapping against the hull. Then a voice rang out.

It came from neither the pirate ship nor Bran's. It seemed to rise from the sea itself, deep and slow, so powerful that she could not tell where it was speaking from.

The words were unfamiliar to Jana.

She looked at Bran. All trace of the old dwarf's smile was gone.

“What is it?”

He did not answer.

The water rose between the two ships.

Something enormous was moving beneath the surface. A curved shape covered in plates emerged amid the foam. For a moment, Jana could make out neither head nor limbs: only a shell so vast that she could not understand how any creature could carry it.

Then a dark mass rose after it.

Sailors stumbled backwards. On the other ship, the pirates were running from one end of the deck to the other. The fighting was over; no one was thinking of renewing the boarding assault.

“Captain!” a sailor shouted.

Bran grabbed a rope to keep his balance.

“Get away from the rail!”

A movement swept through the water, as though the whole sea were trying to turn itself over.

The Wisdom of the Seas rose.

Jana felt the deck fall away beneath her feet. She struck the rail and held on with all her strength. The lanterns swung, casting enormous shadows across the sails.

Farther away, the pirate ship was already turning onto a different course. Men hurried to free the ropes and manoeuvre to escape. Jana caught one last glimpse of the motionless figure on its deck.

Then the creature moved between them, hiding him from her sight.

“Sagesse!” Adwayria cried.

The wolf had slipped on the planks. Bran reached her, bent over her, then raised his head towards his guests.

“We have to abandon ship!”

He pointed towards the stern, where the crew were trying to reach the lifesaving equipment.

“You have to make it to Syndra! Do you hear me?”

Jana tried to move closer.

Bran glanced towards the place on the deck where Doran still lay. Then he turned to the sailors who were still at their posts.

“Come with us!”

“Passengers first! Get them down!” Bran ordered.

Another impact cut them off.

This time, the sound was like nothing Jana had ever heard. A long, deep crack, a howl of timber being torn apart.

The hull gave way.

---

## The Last Glimpse

*At that same moment · aboard the Wisdom of the Seas*

Jana lost her grip.

The deck tilted so violently that she slid several paces before striking an obstacle. She reached out, found a rope and clung to it.

Water was surging up between the split planks.

Someone shouted her name.

She searched for Adwayria, then Zarraï and Erdan. Figures passed in front of her, vanished behind a torn sail, then appeared for an instant in the lantern light.

There was no longer a line to defend, no enemy to drive back. Only the ship coming apart around them.

Jana caught sight of Bran near the mast. A patch of white moved at his side.

“Bran!” Jana shouted.

He raised an arm.

Jana would never know whether he meant to answer her or point to a way out.

A wave crashed over the deck.

She had just enough time to close her eyes before the water struck her. Her hand slipped from the rope. Cold enveloped her, followed by a muffled roar.

She tried to swim upwards.

There was no longer up or down, only bubbles, debris, and the sensation of a current too powerful to let her choose a direction.

She opened her eyes. A glimmer danced somewhere above her.

She thought of Celeborn.

Of Port Nyanzaru, which she had never reached.

Of her three companions, whose fate she could not know.

She reached towards the light.

Then she saw nothing more.
